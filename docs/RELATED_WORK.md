## Paper-ready text

### Generalist Robot Policies and Action Generation

Large-scale robot learning has progressed from task-specific behavioral cloning toward generalist policies trained across tasks, scenes, and embodiments. RT-1 demonstrated that a transformer policy can absorb a large real-robot dataset, while RT-2 transferred Internet-scale visual-language knowledge to control by representing actions as tokens in a vision-language model \citep{brohan2023rt1,brohan2023rt2}. Open X-Embodiment and RT-X then showed positive transfer from standardized cross-embodiment data, and Octo exposed a flexible open-source policy that can be adapted to new observation and action spaces \citep{oneill2023openx,octo2024}. OpenVLA similarly made large VLA pretraining and efficient downstream adaptation accessible \citep{kim2024openvla}. These works establish the data and semantic backbone of generalist manipulation, but their primary supervision is still successful demonstration data.

The action decoder has evolved in parallel. ACT predicts temporally coherent action chunks for fine-grained bimanual manipulation, and Diffusion Policy models multimodal action distributions through conditional denoising \citep{zhao2023act,chi2023diffusionpolicy}. RDT-1B scales diffusion-based action generation across heterogeneous bimanual data, $\pi_0$ couples a pretrained VLM with flow matching, and GR00T N1 uses a vision-language system followed by a diffusion action system \citep{liu2025rdt,black2024pi0,nvidia2025grootn1}. $\pi_{0.5}$ broadens open-world generalization through co-training, while $\pi_{0.7}$ demonstrates that subgoals, task-performance metadata, and other context can steer a generalist policy \citep{intelligence2025pi05,intelligence2026pi07}. FITWAM therefore does not claim metadata conditioning, action tokens, or flow-based control as new. Its target is narrower: convert failed interaction trajectories into action-side negative supervision without treating failed actions as demonstrations or requiring an outcome label at deployment.

### World Models and World Action Models

Predictive models have long been used to connect visual dynamics and robot control. Action-conditioned video prediction can learn physical interaction from unlabeled image transitions, and visual foresight uses predicted frames inside model-predictive control \citep{finn2016video,finn2017foresight}. DayDreamer instead learns latent dynamics for online robot reinforcement learning, whereas UniPi and RoboDreamer formulate language-conditioned future generation as a planning or policy interface \citep{wu2022daydreamer,du2023unipi,zhou2024robodreamer}. These systems support the premise that future-state prediction carries action-relevant physical information, but they typically retain imagined rollouts or planning in the control loop.

Recent world action models jointly learn visual dynamics and robot actions. Motus unifies multiple understanding, generation, inverse-dynamics, and action modes with mixture-of-transformers experts; LingBot-VA uses causal video-action diffusion and asynchronous execution; and DreamZero initializes a joint world-action policy from video diffusion pretraining \citep{bi2025motus,li2026lingbotva,ye2026dreamzero}. Fast-WAM isolates a different regime: future-video supervision is retained during training, while deployment directly samples actions without explicitly generating future video \citep{yuan2026fastwam}. GigaWorld-Policy, Light-WAM, LaWAM, and Being-H0.7 likewise explore action-centered, compact, or latent alternatives to dense test-time video generation \citep{ye2026gigaworld,li2026lightwam,chen2026lawam,luo2026beingh07}. Consequently, action-only WAM inference is an inherited efficiency property of FITWAM rather than its novelty.

Another line compresses world knowledge into structured intermediate variables. World Guidance predicts action-relevant conditions instead of full future observations; OA-WAM exposes object-addressable world states; ImageWAM replaces full video generation with image editing; and Bridge-WA predicts future change and motion representations that guide action generation \citep{su2026worldguidance,liu2026oawam,zhang2026imagewam,bai2026bridgewa}. WALL-WM changes the temporal unit itself by organizing task, subtask, action, and segment supervision around semantic events \citep{li2026wallwm}. FITWAM draws on this event-centered view to avoid contrasting unrelated phases of a task, but adds a distinct problem: locating the short failure-relevant interval inside an episode and using it to learn a deployable success-directed action steer.

### Imperfect Demonstrations, Negative Guidance, and Temporal Credit

Learning from mixed-quality data has traditionally been framed as reward inference, data selection, or weighted imitation. T-REX and D-REX infer preferences from ranked or automatically degraded trajectories and then optimize beyond the demonstrator \citep{brown2019trex,brown2019drex}. ORIL and DemoDICE combine expert demonstrations with unlabeled or supplementary experience through reward or occupancy estimation, while DWBC learns a discriminator-derived weighting for behavioral cloning \citep{zolna2020oril,kim2022demodice,xu2022dwbc}. SSDF is especially close to the present data regime: it assigns quality scores to segments from imperfect robot demonstrations and retains useful segments for downstream policy learning \citep{wu2025ssdf}. These methods show that failed episodes contain both reusable behavior and harmful deviations; an episode-level failure label should therefore not be copied to every action window.

Negative generative guidance offers a second precedent. Contrastive Diffuser pushes plans toward high-return states and away from low-return states \citep{shan2025contradiff}. AFIL most directly models successful and failed robot actions with separate generators and applies adaptive negative guidance during diffusion or flow sampling, whereas FAR builds failure-contrastive preferences inside a test-time retry and continual-improvement loop \citep{zheng2026afil,hao2026far}. FITWAM differs in deployment and supervision: failed actions receive zero behavior-cloning or flow-matching weight, a single action generator is deployed, and failure-derived information is compressed offline into a steer representation rather than recomputed through a failure generator, critic, or retry controller at inference.

The remaining challenge is temporal credit assignment. Weakly supervised temporal localization treats a labeled video as a bag of unlabeled snippets and learns which snippets explain the bag label; UntrimmedNet and RTFM are representative selection- and multiple-instance-learning formulations \citep{wang2017untrimmed,tian2021rtfm}. This perspective is directly relevant when only episode-level robot outcomes are available: a failed episode may contain a long correct prefix, one short erroneous interaction, and a static terminal tail. FITWAM therefore separates a manually localized oracle setting from a weakly supervised soft failure localizer. The latter is accepted as part of the main method only if it localizes held-out manually annotated intervals and survives controls for episode length, collector checkpoint, task identity, and terminal stasis.

### Robot Post-training and Self-improvement

Interactive imitation learning reduces covariate shift by querying experts or collecting corrective data. DAgger aggregates on-policy states with expert labels, DART injects disturbances to obtain corrective demonstrations, and HG-DAgger and ThriftyDAgger allocate human intervention according to risk or novelty \citep{ross2011dagger,laskey2017dart,kelly2019hgdagger,hoque2022thriftydagger}. RoboCat scales a related self-improvement loop by generating additional robot data for selected tasks \citep{bousmalis2023robocat}. These approaches motivate iterative data collection, but they rely on expert relabeling, intervention, or repeated deployment rather than extracting a negative training signal from already recorded failures.

Robot post-training increasingly combines demonstrations with autonomous experience and reinforcement learning. RLPD and SERL provide practical recipes for online RL from offline initialization, and DPPO directly optimizes diffusion policies with reinforcement learning \citep{ball2023rlpd,luo2025serl,ren2024dppo}. RECAP, used by $\pi^{*}_{0.6}$, combines demonstrations, autonomous experience, corrections, and advantage conditioning; RL Token exposes a compact interface for online VLA adaptation \citep{intelligence2025pi06star,xu2026rltoken}. RISE scores imagined rollouts with a progress value model, while AdaJEPA updates latent world dynamics from observed transitions during plan-execute-adapt-replan operation \citep{yang2026rise,wang2026adajepa}. FITWAM's offline stage requires none of these online rewards or test-time updates, although its steer token and EveRobot data provenance are designed to support a later online phase.

Failure reasoning and recovery are complementary but different objectives. AHA detects and explains manipulation failures, and REFLECT summarizes experience to support language-based correction \citep{duan2024aha,liu2023reflect}. Such systems ask what went wrong and how to retry. FITWAM first asks a smaller causal question: can failed action intervals improve the next offline policy update when they are used only as negatives? Failure diagnosis, language critiques, retry, and online policy improvement remain later modules and should not be used to explain gains in the offline ablation.

### Tactile Representation and Tactile World Modeling

Touch provides a direct observation of contact that can be ambiguous or invisible in RGB. DIGIT made compact high-resolution vision-based tactile sensing broadly accessible, Touch and Go paired human-collected visual and tactile experience, and TacSL enables scalable visuotactile simulation and policy learning \citep{lambeta2020digit,yang2022touchgo,akinola2025tacsl}. Sparsh and AnyTouch learn reusable representations across tactile tasks or sensors, while Sparsh-X fuses image, audio, motion, and pressure through bottleneck tokens \citep{higuera2024sparsh,feng2025anytouch,higuera2025sparshx}. FTP-1 further studies a generalist tactile policy across sensors and embodiments \citep{yuan2026ftp1}. These works provide encoders and compact token interfaces for FITWAM, but do not themselves define failure-aware steering.

Tactile information has also entered end-to-end policies and predictive models. Reactive Diffusion Policy combines slower visual-tactile action chunks with fast tactile feedback, and VTLA uses tactile-language-action modeling with preference learning for insertion \citep{xue2025rdp,zhang2025vtla}. Visuo-Tactile World Models predict future visual and tactile latents for planning, OmniVTA combines tactile representation, short-horizon contact prediction, policy fusion, and a high-frequency feedback path, and VTAM jointly models video, tactile, and action streams \citep{higuera2026vtwm,zheng2026omnivta,yuan2026vtam}. Dream-Tac, Tactile-WAM, and VT-WAM further study joint tactile futures, asymmetric tactile attention, or contact-gated tactile guidance in WAMs \citep{lou2026dreamtac,wu2026tactilewam,tian2026vtwam}. The final FITWAM paper should retain this subsection only if tactile enters both the method and experiments. Its defensible tactile contribution would be using contact-localized failure signals to sharpen negative supervision, not introducing tactile WAMs as a category.

## Positioning boundary

The narrow claim to test is:

> Offline failed trajectories improve a Fast-WAM policy when failure-relevant action intervals supervise a success-directed steering representation, while failed actions are not behavior-cloned and inference remains one-pass and action-only.

Avoid claims of being the first WAM, action-only WAM, event-based WAM, metadata-conditioned policy, tactile WAM, failure-aware robot learner, or autonomous recovery system. AFIL is the closest mechanism-level comparison; FAR is the closest failure-contrast and continual-recovery comparison; WALL-WM is the closest event-data comparison; $\pi_{0.7}$ is the closest metadata-steering comparison; and Tactile-WAM/VT-WAM are the closest tactile architecture comparisons.

If the submitted paper remains offline and vision-only, remove the tactile subsection from the main paper and keep it in future work. The bibliography may remain broader than the final prose so that the section can be updated when the tactile experiments are complete.
