# FITWAM Related Work

The bibliography is maintained in [`fitwam_related_work.bib`](./fitwam_related_work.bib).

## World-Action Models

World-action models (WAMs) augment visuomotor policies with predictive objectives that model how observations evolve under action. Existing designs either generate visual futures before action prediction or jointly model future video and actions in a shared generative process. UVA and UWM establish representative unified formulations in which video and action prediction share a learned world representation \citep{li2025uva,zhu2025uwm}. Fast-WAM isolates a complementary finding: much of this benefit can be retained by co-training on future video while directly predicting actions at deployment, without test-time video synthesis \citep{yuan2026fastwam}. AGRA further shows that visually plausible predictions are insufficient when their hidden representations are poorly organized for low-level control \citep{qiu2026agra}. FITWAM adopts Fast-WAM's action-only inference setting and asks how failed interactions can shape an action-relevant world representation.

Two recent directions are especially relevant to this question. WALL-WM replaces arbitrary fixed clips with semantically coherent task and interaction events, providing a natural temporal unit for localizing where an episode succeeds or fails \citep{li2026wallwm}. World Pilot steers a VLA with latent and action priors extracted from a WAM, demonstrating that predictive representations can influence action generation through a compact interface \citep{lin2026worldpilot}. FITWAM combines these ideas at a different point in the learning loop: it derives an action-side steer from event-local success and failure trajectories. Failed actions are not used as imitation targets, and the deployed policy does not require a generated future or a failure-conditioned model.

## Learning from Failed Robot Experience

Behavior cloning normally treats demonstrations as uniformly desirable, which makes direct training on autonomous rollouts unsafe. Prior work instead extracts useful supervision from imperfect experience. SSDF estimates the quality of trajectory segments and reuses high-quality portions of imperfect demonstrations \citep{wu2025ssdf}. $\pi_{0.7}$ trains on heterogeneous robot data annotated with task-performance metadata, allowing desired behavior to be selected through context at inference time \citep{intelligence2026pi07}. These approaches show that imperfect data become useful when quality is represented locally or explicitly, but neither uses failed action segments as negative supervision for a WAM action expert.

The closest comparisons explicitly exploit failure. DALI-R learns latent dynamics from suboptimal and failed trajectories, then imagines and reranks candidate action chunks during inference \citep{luo2026dalir}. AFIL learns successful and failed action generators and guides sampling away from the failure distribution at deployment \citep{zheng2026afil}. FITWAM studies a simpler offline interface. EveRobot records rollout provenance, outcomes, and interaction-event boundaries; failure actions receive no behavior-cloning or flow-matching target; and localized success and failure chunks train a contrastive representation that conditions the action expert through a success-directed steer token. At inference time, FITWAM uses a single action generator with no outcome prompt, failure generator, critic, reranking stage, retry controller, or online update. The comparison therefore tests whether failure information can be distilled into the policy during offline training rather than consulted again at deployment.

## Tactile Learning for Contact-Rich Manipulation

Touch exposes contact state, force, and slip that can remain ambiguous in RGB observations. Sparsh learns reusable self-supervised tactile representations, while Sparsh-X compresses complementary visual, audio, motion, and pressure cues through multisensory bottleneck tokens \citep{higuera2024sparsh,higuera2025sparshx}. FTP-1 further scales tactile representations into a generalist policy across sensors and contact-rich tasks \citep{yuan2026ftp1}. These works motivate a compact tactile interface rather than treating raw tactile streams as additional image channels.

Predictive tactile models are the closest architectural line. VTAM jointly models video, touch, and action for complex physical interaction, and Tactile-WAM incorporates tactile signals into a WAM through asymmetric cross-modal attention \citep{yuan2026vtam,wu2026tactilewam}. Their primary contribution is tactile-aware prediction and control. FITWAM instead studies tactile feedback as evidence for failure-localized self-improvement: contact events identify where an interaction departs from successful behavior, and the resulting signal updates the success-directed action representation. The full claim therefore requires showing that failure-aware tactile training improves over both a success-only Fast-WAM and a tactile WAM trained without failed trajectories.

## Positioning

FITWAM uses event-local failed trajectories as action-side negative supervision to learn a success-directed steer for a Fast-WAM policy, without imitating failed actions or requiring failure context at deployment.
