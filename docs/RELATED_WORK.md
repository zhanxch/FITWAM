# FITWAM Related Work

The bibliography is maintained in [`fitwam_related_work.bib`](./fitwam_related_work.bib).

## World-Action Models and Video Supervision

Vision-language-action policies such as RT-2, OpenVLA, and $\pi_{0.5}$ map visual and language context directly to robot actions \citep{brohan2023rt2,kim2024openvla,intelligence2025pi05}. World-action models add an explicit future-observation objective. UVA decouples video and action heads and can bypass video generation during action inference; UWM couples video and action diffusion while retaining modality-specific noise levels, which lets the same model consume action-free video; and Motus uses a Mixture-of-Transformers to support video, action, and joint prediction modes \citep{li2025uva,zhu2025uwm,bi2025motus}. DreamZero is the central large-scale reference for the recent WAM formulation: a pretrained video diffusion backbone jointly models future video and actions, and video-only human or cross-embodiment demonstrations improve control without action labels \citep{ye2026dreamzero}. Fast-WAM is the immediate basis of FITWAM. It shows that the video objective remains useful during training even when future-video generation is removed at deployment \citep{yuan2026fastwam}.

This separation is particularly useful for failed rollouts. A standard VLA objective has no future-video term to retain once failed actions are removed from behavior cloning; a WAM can keep the failed sample's video objective while setting its direct action-imitation weight to zero. This is an objective-level advantage, not a claim that VLA architectures are fundamentally unable to add auxiliary video losses. Under Fast-WAM's directional attention mask, failed-sample video loss updates the Video Expert rather than the Action Expert parameters; deployed actions may still change because action queries consume first-frame video K/V features. Failure video therefore adapts the world representation but does not by itself provide a success direction. FITWAM adds that direction through an explicit action-side steer while preserving action-only deployment.

## Learning from Imperfect and Failed Robot Experience

Imperfect trajectories become useful when their quality is represented locally rather than treating every action as an expert target. Ergodic Imitation learns task information from both positive and negative demonstrations, and offline imitation from undesirable demonstrations explicitly separates expert and undesirable visitation distributions \citep{kalinowska2021ergodic,hoang2025undesirable}. SSDF uses self-supervision to recover useful experience from imperfect demonstrations, while $\pi_{0.7}$ conditions a generalist policy on performance metadata and other steering context that remains available at inference \citep{wu2025ssdf,intelligence2026pi07}. Methods that use failures more directly often add inference-time machinery: DALI-R learns latent dynamics from mixed-quality trajectories and reranks imagined action chunks, while AFIL trains success and failure action generators and applies negative guidance during sampling \citep{luo2026dalir,zheng2026afil}.

FITWAM instead distills failure information during offline training and deploys one policy without an outcome label, failure generator, or reranker. WALL-WM provides the event granularity used to avoid pairing arbitrary fixed clips \citep{li2026wallwm}. The action-trajectory teacher treats phase-matched successful actions as positives and failed actions as negatives; a current-observation student predicts the resulting success-directed steer for the Action Expert. Privileged teacher-student training provides the general precedent for using richer information only during training \citep{messikommer2025sitt}. The representation objective draws on supervised contrastive learning and on CLASS, which uses action-sequence similarity to supervise robot representations \citep{khosla2020supcon,lee2025class}. These are component precedents, but none establishes the proposed event-paired steer for a WAM. Compact bottleneck queries follow the information-compression principle of attention bottlenecks and Sparsh-X rather than serving as evidence for a failure direction \citep{nagrani2021attention,higuera2025sparshx}.

## Tactile Representations and Tactile World-Action Models

Touch exposes force, slip, and contact transitions that can remain ambiguous in RGB. Sparsh learns reusable self-supervised tactile features; Sparsh-X fuses image, audio, motion, and pressure into a unified multisensory representation; and FTP-1 scales tactile pretraining across sensors and downstream policies \citep{higuera2024sparsh,higuera2025sparshx,yuan2026ftp1}. These works establish strong tactile encoders and motivate a compact tactile interface.

Recent tactile world-action models integrate touch into predictive control. VTAM grounds video-action modeling with tactile streams, Dream-Tac jointly models visual, tactile, and action dynamics with contact-aware fusion, and Tactile-WAM protects visual prediction while exposing tactile evidence to the action stream through asymmetric attention \citep{yuan2026vtam,lou2026dreamtac,wu2026tactilewam}. VT-WAM similarly combines future visual and tactile prediction with contact-gated action guidance \citep{tian2026vtwam}. These systems establish that tactile dynamics benefit contact-rich manipulation. The present offline study addresses the orthogonal problem of learning from localized success and failure interaction events; tactile self-improvement is outside its evaluated scope.

## Positioning

FITWAM studies whether phase-matched failed action events can supervise an observation-conditioned steer for a Fast-WAM Action Expert, without directly imitating failed actions or requiring failure context at deployment.
