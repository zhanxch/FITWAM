# FITWAM Related Work

The bibliography is maintained in [`fitwam_related_work.bib`](./fitwam_related_work.bib).

## World-Action Models and Video Supervision

Vision-language-action policies such as RT-2, OpenVLA, and $\pi_{0.5}$ map visual and language context directly to robot actions \citep{brohan2023rt2,kim2024openvla,intelligence2025pi05}. World-action models (WAMs) additionally learn future observation dynamics. DreamZero scales this direction by adapting a pretrained video diffusion model to jointly predict video and action, showing that action-free human and cross-embodiment video can improve robot control \citep{ye2026dreamzero}. UVA separates video and action prediction heads, UWM couples their diffusion processes with modality-specific noise levels, and Motus uses a Mixture-of-Transformers for video, action, and joint prediction \citep{li2025uva,zhu2025uwm,bi2025motus}. Fast-WAM is the architectural basis of FITWAM: it retains joint world-action training while removing future-video generation from deployment \citep{yuan2026fastwam}.

These WAM objectives expose a direct route for video without action supervision: UWM masks missing actions, and DreamZero applies a video-only objective to cross-embodiment clips. In FITWAM's Fast-WAM training path, a failed rollout still carries its logged action tensor, but its action loss is set to zero while video flow-matching supervision is retained. The directional attention mask prevents this video loss from training the Action Expert parameters directly; the adapted representation can still affect control because action queries read first-frame video K/V features. An unchanged action-only VLA objective has no corresponding temporal loss, while adding visual-prediction or world-model training creates such a path. Failure video supplies transition evidence; successful actions and explicit steering supply the control direction.

## Offline Learning from Failed Experience

Mixed-quality robot data require a learning rule that distinguishes useful state transitions from undesirable actions. Ergodic Imitation and offline imitation from undesirable demonstrations model positive and negative behavior separately, while SSDF recovers supervision from imperfect demonstrations \citep{kalinowska2021ergodic,hoang2025undesirable,wu2025ssdf}. Recent generalist policies expose stronger steering interfaces: $\pi_{0.7}$ conditions behavior on performance metadata, DALI-R reranks imagined action chunks learned from mixed-quality trajectories, and AFIL applies negative guidance from a failure action model during sampling \citep{intelligence2026pi07,luo2026dalir,zheng2026afil}. These approaches either retain steering context or add inference-time selection machinery.

FITWAM's offline design distills localized failure information into a single deployable policy. Interaction events provide the unit of comparison, following the event-level organization of WALL-WM \citep{li2026wallwm}. A training-only trajectory teacher encodes phase-matched successful and failed action events; an observation-conditioned student predicts a success-directed steer token for the Action Expert. This follows the broader principle of privileged teacher-student learning, while supervised contrastive learning and action-sequence-supervised representation learning provide the relevant objectives \citep{messikommer2025sitt,khosla2020supcon,lee2025class}. Compact bottleneck queries provide an information bottleneck for event trajectories rather than a hand-specified arithmetic direction \citep{nagrani2021attention,higuera2025sparshx}. Failed actions are contrastive evidence, never direct action-imitation targets.

## Online Policy and World-Model Improvement

Online adaptation must improve precision without destabilizing a pretrained policy. Residual reinforcement learning adds a learned correction to a competent base controller; PLD uses residual actors to probe VLA failure regions and distills the resulting recovery data \citep{johannink2019residual,xiao2025pld}. RECAP combines demonstrations, on-policy experience, and interventions through advantage-conditioned VLA post-training, while RL Token exposes a compact VLA representation to a lightweight actor-critic anchored to the base policy \citep{intelligence2025recap,xu2026rltoken}. FITWAM's online design transfers this compact interface to interaction-event granularity. An event-conditioned readout feeds the actor-critic, and event-local rewards train a bounded correction over the frozen Fast-WAM action. The offline steer can condition this actor but remains a separate, fixed representation. Synchronous and versioned asynchronous updates share the same event-indexed replay contract.

Predicted futures provide a complementary route for improving both action and world representations. WoG injects compressed future-observation conditions into action generation, AdaWAM dynamically routes between action-only and multimodal reasoning, and KeyWorld concentrates generation on motion-defined keyframes \citep{su2026wog,tang2026adawam,li2025keyworld}. AdaJEPA updates a latent world model from observed transitions during MPC; AdaWorldPolicy uses visual and force prediction errors for online adaptation of coupled world, action, and force modules \citep{wang2026adajepa,yuan2026adaworldpolicy}. The proposed FITWAM online branch separates guidance from calibration. At a critical interaction transition, a subgoal head predicts a compact future condition that guides the Action Expert. A distinct transition head predicts the consequence of the action actually executed; the later observation provides its self-supervised World Expert update. Both paths share the event index, and the tactile stage extends their targets from vision to contact.

## Tactile Representations and Tactile World-Action Models

Touch exposes force, slip, and contact transitions that can remain ambiguous in RGB. Sparsh learns reusable self-supervised tactile features; Sparsh-X fuses image, audio, motion, and pressure through compact bottleneck tokens; and FTP-1 scales tactile pretraining across sensors and downstream policies \citep{higuera2024sparsh,higuera2025sparshx,yuan2026ftp1}. These models motivate a compact tactile interface for event-level prediction and steering.

Recent tactile WAMs integrate touch into predictive control. VTAM models video, tactile signals, and actions; Dream-Tac jointly predicts visual-tactile-action dynamics; Tactile-WAM uses asymmetric attention to expose tactile evidence to the action stream; and VT-WAM combines future visual and tactile prediction with contact-gated action guidance \citep{yuan2026vtam,lou2026dreamtac,wu2026tactilewam,tian2026vtwam}. OmniVTA closes the loop with high-rate correction from predicted-versus-observed tactile signals, while TouchWorld separates subtask planning, tactile subgoal prediction, nominal action generation, and tactile residual refinement \citep{zheng2026omnivta,zhou2026touchworld}. FITWAM proposes the shared interaction event as the interface for offline success/failure contrast, online residual credit, and adaptive World Expert updates without treating failed actions as demonstrations.

## Positioning

FITWAM is structured around two uses of interaction experience. Its offline design uses phase-matched failed action events to supervise an observation-conditioned steer without outcome labels at deployment. Its online design uses event-local reinforcement for reference-conditioned action refinement; a predicted subgoal guides action, while an action-conditioned predicted-versus-observed transition updates the World Expert. The tactile stage extends both paths to contact signals.
