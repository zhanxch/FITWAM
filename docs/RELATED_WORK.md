# FITWAM Related Work

The bibliography is maintained in [`fitwam_related_work.bib`](./fitwam_related_work.bib).

## World-Action Models

World-action models (WAMs) augment visuomotor policies with predictive objectives that model how observations evolve under action. Existing designs either generate visual futures before action prediction or jointly model future video and actions in a shared generative process. UVA learns a shared video-action representation with decoupled heads and bypasses video generation during action inference, while UWM couples video and action diffusion in a unified transformer \citep{li2025uva,zhu2025uwm}. Fast-WAM provides the controlled finding that video co-training can retain most of its control benefit even when future generation is removed at deployment \citep{yuan2026fastwam}. AGRA further shows that visually plausible predictions are insufficient when their hidden representations are poorly organized for low-level control \citep{qiu2026agra}. FITWAM builds on this training-time world supervision and asks how failed interactions can shape an action-relevant representation.

Two recent directions are especially relevant to this question. WALL-WM replaces arbitrary fixed clips with semantically coherent events, establishing event-aligned supervision across language, vision, and action \citep{li2026wallwm}. FITWAM adapts this event unit as a hypothesis for localizing failure-relevant interaction intervals. World Pilot steers a VLA with latent and action priors extracted from a WAM, demonstrating that predictive representations can influence action generation through a compact interface \citep{lin2026worldpilot}. The proposed offline method instead learns an action-side steer from matched success and failure events. Failed actions are excluded from direct imitation targets, and deployment requires neither a generated future nor a failure-conditioned model.

## Robot Post-training from Failed Experience

Behavior cloning normally treats demonstrations as uniformly desirable, so naive training on autonomous rollouts can reinforce failed or suboptimal actions. Prior work instead extracts useful supervision from imperfect experience. SSDF estimates the quality of trajectory segments and reuses high-quality portions of imperfect demonstrations \citep{wu2025ssdf}. $\pi_{0.7}$ trains on heterogeneous robot data annotated with task-performance metadata, allowing desired behavior to be selected through context at inference time \citep{intelligence2026pi07}. These approaches show that imperfect data become useful when quality is represented locally or explicitly, but neither uses failed action segments as negative supervision for a WAM action expert.

The closest comparisons explicitly exploit failure. DALI-R learns latent dynamics from suboptimal and failed trajectories, then imagines and reranks candidate action chunks during inference \citep{luo2026dalir}. AFIL learns successful and failed action generators and guides sampling away from the failure distribution at deployment \citep{zheng2026afil}. FITWAM isolates a training-only alternative: the proposed objective disables direct action targets for failed samples and contrastively learns a success-directed action steer from matched events. Unlike DALI-R and AFIL, deployment uses no reranking stage or second failure generator. This comparison tests whether offline training can distill failure information into one deployed policy rather than consulting a failure model again at inference.

## Tactile Learning for Contact-Rich Manipulation

Touch exposes contact state, force, and slip that can remain ambiguous in RGB observations. Sparsh learns reusable self-supervised tactile representations, while Sparsh-X compresses complementary visual, audio, motion, and pressure cues through multisensory bottleneck tokens \citep{higuera2024sparsh,higuera2025sparshx}. FTP-1 further scales tactile representations into a generalist policy across sensors and contact-rich tasks \citep{yuan2026ftp1}. These works motivate a compact tactile interface rather than treating raw tactile streams as additional image channels.

Tactile-conditioned action models are the closest architectural line. VTAM uses tactile perception as a grounding stream for video-action modeling, while Tactile-WAM models touch changes and integrates them through asymmetric cross-modal attention \citep{yuan2026vtam,wu2026tactilewam}. FITWAM's planned tactile stage treats contact feedback as evidence for failure-localized self-improvement: contact events may reveal where an interaction departs from successful behavior and guide an update to the action representation. This claim will require showing that failure-aware tactile training improves over both a success-only Fast-WAM and a tactile WAM trained without failed trajectories.

## Positioning

FITWAM studies whether event-local failed trajectories can provide action-side negative supervision for a Fast-WAM policy without imitating failed actions or requiring failure context at deployment.
